Public Interest Brief · April 2026

Alberta's disability supports are
breaking under their own rules.

AISH, ADAP, FSCD, PDD, primary care, appeals — each system is failing in a different way, and together they form a single trap. This site gathers the evidence, the arithmetic, and the flyers you can print and share.

⏳ Time-sensitive — act now

Appeal an AISH decision

The window to appeal an existing AISH decision closes June 30. The government accepts a signed written letter, so you don't need their online form. The appeal is already written — you just add your details. If you receive CPP Disability, there's a stronger version that appeals on those grounds.

Open fillable Print & sign ★ On CPP Disability? Use this stronger appeal

Request your own AISH file

Get a free copy of your own AISH file through Access to Information, so you can see what's on record and catch any gaps before a reassessment or appeal. They have 30 days to respond, so request early.

Open ATIA form

Government form — save it, then open in Adobe Reader. It will not open inside a web browser.

Fix a wrong $200 CDB deduction

Your cheque can be docked $200 for the Canada Disability Benefit even if you were denied it, are still waiting, or were approved for less than the full amount. If that $200 came off but doesn't match your real CDB status, this letter tells AISH to deduct only what you actually receive.

Open letter

Got a move letter by mistake?

If you're 60 or older, in continuing care, terminally ill, or have a developmental disability or PDD eligibility, you stay on AISH automatically. If you still received an ADAP move letter, that's a coding error — this request asks them to correct your category and keep you on AISH.

Open request

The System

Four failures. One trap.

Each program below is failing on its own terms. Read each pillar — and then read how they interact, because they were never designed to interact at all.

I

Income — AISH to ADAP

79,290 Albertans on AISH are being moved to a new program on July 1, 2026. The eligibility criteria for ADAP have not been published. Bill 12 removed independent appeal rights. The $200/mo federal Canada Disability Benefit is clawed back dollar-for-dollar — the only province in Canada to do this.

Financial Reality Report →

II

Health — Coverage Erosion

Most psychotherapy is not covered by AHCIP or AISH benefits. Psychiatry waits run 6–18+ months. 650,000 Albertans have no family doctor. The healthcare worker shortage compounds every other system in this list.

Health Benefits Erosion Report →

III

Children — FSCD & PDD

The same families being moved off AISH have children on the FSCD/PDD waitlists. Average wait: three years. Intakes have been quietly closing. The disability income system and the children's disability service system are failing the same households simultaneously.

Three Years and Counting Report →

IV

Rights — Appeals & Caseworkers

Bill 12 removed independent appeal rights for eligibility decisions. Caseworker authority expanded. The Alberta Human Rights Commission is still available, but the front-line mechanism most disabled Albertans actually use is gone.

Caseworker Appeal Rights Report →

Appeals

What Bill 12 actually changed.

Before December 9, 2025, an Albertan denied AISH could appeal to an independent body. Bill 12 removed that. Eligibility decisions for AISH and ADAP now sit with the Minister, with no independent reviewer between you and the program.

The reports linked here document the change clause-by-clause, examine what's left of the appeal process, and lay out the Charter mobility-rights argument and the constitutional analysis.

Pre-Appeal Submission of New Information

Ask AISH to reconsider a recent decision before you file a Notice of Appeal. Critical: once a Notice of Appeal is filed, the AISH Policy Manual prohibits the Appeal Panel from considering any new information — the pre-appeal stage is the only window where new evidence can change the outcome. Does not pause the 30-day appeal clock.

Open form

AISH Appeal Letter

A formal Notice of Appeal letter for an AISH decision — fillable to complete on screen, or a ready-to-print-and-sign version. File within 30 days of being notified of the decision and your right to appeal. See the plain-language appeal guides in Documents for which route fits your situation.

AISH Appeal on CPP-D Grounds

If you have been approved for the Canada Pension Plan Disability benefit, this letter appeals your AISH decision and asks to remain on AISH on those grounds. CPP-D's federal test — severe and prolonged — maps directly onto the AISH severe-handicap test, so your CPP-D approval is strong evidence already on the record. Fill in the blanks, sign, and attach your CPP-D approval letter.

CDB Clawback Documentation

Document the $200/month CDB clawback even if your federal amount is less than $200, or hasn't arrived yet. Each completed form is a documented receipt — the pattern is what gets the system to fix it. Includes a full directory of every Alberta Supports / AISH office.

Open form

Monthly Income Status Report

Self-declaration that fulfils your monthly AISH/ADAP reporting obligation in writing — in months you worked, didn't work, or had any status change. A paper trail protects your file when phone or email reports go missing or unrecorded. Cites the Government of Alberta's own reporting requirement.

Open form

AISH File Correction Letter

For when AISH has processed your monthly benefit incorrectly because previously-reported information was not recorded in your file — benefit reduced or held to $0, paystubs lost, status changes never entered. Formal written request for file correction citing your reporting obligation under Government of Alberta policy.

Open letter

Exemption Category Correction Request

For anyone — or anyone you care for — in one of the four groups the government's own rules say automatically remain on AISH (a severe/profound developmental disability or PDD eligibility, a palliative or terminal condition, continuing care, or age 60+) who received an ADAP letter anyway. This is not an appeal — it's a request to correct an administrative error and to be told, in writing, who made the decision and why. Section 12.8 removed the appeal for the transition; it did not remove your place in these categories.

Open form

CDB $200 Deduction Correction Letter

For when AISH or ADAP deducted the $200 Canada Disability Benefit amount but the deduction doesn't match the CDB status you reported — your application was denied and the $200 was taken anyway, it's still pending with no decision, or the status you reported wasn't recorded. A formal file-correction request citing your reporting obligation, asking for reversal where the deduction doesn't reflect federal income you actually receive.

Letter to the Minister

A letter anyone can send directly to the Minister responsible for AISH and ADAP.
