Who we are

The Alberta Disability System Breakdown is an informational group, run by me, Chantal Trepanier. I'm a parent and a disability advocate. I read the government's own laws, rules and pages, and explain them in plain language.

What we are NOT:

  • Not the government. We don't work for AISH, ADAP or any ministry.
  • Not lawyers or doctors. We share information, not legal or medical advice.
  • Not a political party. We don't tell anyone how to vote. Members here hold all kinds of views, and everyone's welcome.
  • Not a crisis service. If you're in crisis, see the bottom of this page.

Words you'll see a lot

AISH
Assured Income for the Severely Handicapped. Alberta's income program for people whose disability prevents them from working.
ADAP
The Alberta Disability Assistance Program. It started July 2, 2026, for people the government says can do some work. Many people were moved from AISH to ADAP.
CDB
The Canada Disability Benefit, a federal monthly payment. Alberta counts it as income and takes it off AISH and ADAP payments.
Your local AISH/ADAP office
The office that handles your file. Email addresses are below.
Regulation
The detailed rules made under a law. Most AISH and ADAP rules are in one called AR 96/2026.
Policy manual
The government's instructions to staff on how to apply the rules.
MLA
Your Member of the Legislative Assembly, the person elected to represent your area.

The golden rules

These come up in almost every post, so here's why we say them. You don't have to do any of these alone. Reach out and we'll do them together.

  1. Put it in writing. An email leaves a record of what you asked, when you asked it, and what they said. A phone call doesn't. Writing is also an accommodation for people who can't use the phone. If you do call, send a short email afterward summarizing the call.
  2. Put your full name and file number in the subject line, every time. Your email may not show your name on their end, and a nickname email address doesn't tell them who you are. Your file number is on your letters or your health benefits card.
  3. Keep copies of everything you send and get, with the dates.
  4. Report changes. Tell your office in writing about any change in your income, assets, address, work or household, even when it won't change your payment. Reporting protects you.
  5. Ask for decisions in writing. If they say no, ask for the decision and the reason in writing. You need that to ask for a review or an appeal.
  6. You can ask for accommodations. If your disability makes something hard (like phone calls), you can ask in writing for them to contact you another way, and ask them to put it on your file.
  7. Copy (cc) your MLA when you're stuck. Find yours at elections.ab.ca, under "Find my electoral division."

Your privacy

To protect everyone's privacy, I regularly delete messages and emails on my end. That way, if the worst ever happened and one of my accounts was hacked, your personal information wouldn't be sitting there.

What that means for you:

  • Keep your own copies of what we've talked about, especially letters and emails we wrote together.
  • When you message me about something we talked about before, a quick reminder of the details helps a lot. Something like: "You helped me with my CDB letter last month. Here's what happened next."
  • Please bear with me if I need a moment to remember. I'm one person helping a lot of people, and I'd rather protect your information than keep it.

Group rules

  • Be kind. Everyone here is dealing with a lot.
  • Keep private details private. Don't post your file number, address, health details or anyone else's in public. Send me a private message instead.
  • When members share their stories, I post them without names.
  • No telling people how to vote, and no advertising.
  • If I get something wrong, I'll correct it publicly, in the same place I said it.

When you post a question

Tell us: are you on AISH or ADAP, what happened, and what you've already tried. The more we know, the better we can help.

If you're in crisis

  • Call or text 988 any time, day or night.
  • If you or someone else is in danger, call 911.
