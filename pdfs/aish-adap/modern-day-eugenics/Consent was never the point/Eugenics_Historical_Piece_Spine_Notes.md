## The Five-Part Throughline

The piece traces a single operating principle across five distinct historical and contemporary expressions:

**1. The historical *Sexual Sterilization Act* (1928–1972)**
Decisions made about disabled people without their consent, on the basis that the deciding authority knew best.

**2. The structural eugenics framework (1972–present)**
Policies producing eugenic effects (segregation, social exclusion, prevention of partnership and reproduction, isolation) without requiring eugenic intent. The legal architecture of explicit eugenics was dismantled. The operating premise that disabled people's intimate, relational, and reproductive lives are appropriate sites for state regulation in ways non-disabled people's are not was never specifically repudiated.

**3. The contemporary AISH-to-ADAP transition (2025–2026, Bill 12)**
A fundamental restructuring of disabled people's income security imposed without meeting the consultation standards Canada has formally accepted under the *Convention on the Rights of Persons with Disabilities* (CRPD). Approximately 79,290 Albertans being processed through a transition whose operational rules have not been published.

**4. The information asymmetry condition (the upstream condition for all of the above)**
People cannot consent to what they have not been told. The absence of information functionally invalidates the consent the province claims its programs operate under. *No information, no consent. No consent, no legitimacy.*

**5. Modern Eugenics Momentum: The Institutional Rebuild (2024–present)**
The *Compassionate Intervention Act*, its continental siblings (Saskatchewan, Ontario, BC retreat from harm reduction, US federal executive order), and the construction of $180M in new secure institutional infrastructure in Alberta. The legal framework for non-consensual confinement of people who have not committed a crime is being re-established, with the same compassion rhetoric and the same target populations as the historical eugenic period. This is the move the piece exists to name in real time.

## The Legal and Definitional Framework

### Eugenics — Standard Scholarly Definition

Eugenics is not limited to surgical sterilization. The full scholarly definition (NHGRI, Merriam-Webster, NIH) explicitly includes **segregation and social exclusion** as eugenic mechanisms when their function is to prevent reproduction or eliminate "inferior" traits from the population.

Historical eugenic mechanisms in Alberta included:
- Surgical sterilization (the *Sexual Sterilization Act*)
- Institutional segregation (residential institutions, including the Provincial Training School at Red Deer)
- Marriage prohibitions
- Immigration exclusion
- Cohabitation restrictions in residential institutions
- The active discouragement of disabled people from forming households and families

These were all part of the same operating framework. The *Act* was one tool among many.

### Structural Eugenics

A documented phenomenon in disability studies literature: policies designed for non-eugenic reasons (cost containment, bureaucratic uniformity, fiscal restraint) that produce eugenic outcomes (prevention of disabled reproduction, social exclusion, isolation) and are sustained politically because they produce those outcomes without anyone having to defend them.

The *intent* claim is harder to make than the *effect* claim. Modern AISH cohabitation policy was probably not designed with explicit eugenic intent. But its effect — making domestic partnership functionally impossible at AISH income levels for the partnered disabled adult — falls overwhelmingly on the disabled population and produces the social exclusion that historical eugenics achieved through more explicit means.

### CRPD Article 4(3) — Convention on the Rights of Persons with Disabilities

Canada ratified the CRPD in 2010. Article 4(3) reads:

> "In the development and implementation of legislation and policies to implement the present Convention, and in other decision-making processes concerning issues relating to persons with disabilities, States Parties shall closely consult with and actively involve persons with disabilities, including children with disabilities, through their representative organizations."

Bill 12 was passed December 9, 2025 without consultation that the disability community recognizes as meeting this standard. Inclusion Alberta's November 26, 2025 media release stated explicitly: *"Bill 12 erodes rights and deepens poverty for Albertans with disabilities."* Disability Action Hall, the Alberta Medical Association, and AUPE have publicly opposed the transition. No major Alberta disability advocacy organization has endorsed it.

### Free, Prior, and Informed Consent (FPIC)

The framework Indigenous rights advocacy has built to address the same structural problem in the resource-development context — under UNDRIP Articles 10, 11, 19, 28, 29, and 32. The Alberta government's July 10, 2025 opposition to federal Bill C-61 (First Nations Clean Water Act) is a recent demonstration of the same consultation-failure pattern at work in adjacent jurisdictions of the same provincial administration.

The principle is identical across both contexts: *decisions affecting a defined population's rights, autonomy, or material conditions require the meaningful participation of that population in advance of the decision being made.* Alberta has failed this standard with Indigenous nations and with the disability community simultaneously.

## Contemporary Expressions of Structural Eugenics in Alberta

To be developed in the full piece. Initial inventory:

### AISH Cohabitation Policy
Treats a cohabiting partner's income as household income, which functionally penalizes partnership for AISH recipients at sub-survival benefit levels. Effect: disabled adults are pushed toward isolation, hidden relationships, or disqualification from benefits if they form domestic partnerships. The structure replicates the historical exclusion of disabled people from family formation through economic rather than surgical means.

### PDD Guardianship Structure
Persons with Developmental Disabilities program retains guardianship and decision-making authority structures that limit disabled adults' autonomy in ways non-disabled adults do not experience. Marriage, reproduction, residence, and financial decisions are subject to oversight that historical eugenic frameworks made explicit. The contemporary version makes them administrative.

### Institutional Care Practices
Documented practice of separating couples upon entry to long-term care or supported living arrangements. The historical residential institutions did this explicitly. Contemporary care facilities continue the practice through bed-allocation and room-assignment policies framed as administrative necessity.

### Sub-Survival Income Levels
AISH at $1,940/month and ADAP at $1,740/month, against Alberta median rent of $1,500–$1,900 for a one-bedroom unit, function as a structural exclusion from community participation. The historical eugenic frameworks excluded disabled people from community life through institutionalization. The contemporary framework excludes them through poverty.

### FSCD Three-Year Wait Time
Family Support for Children with Disabilities operates with a documented wait time that exceeds the developmental window during which early intervention is most effective. The structural effect is to ensure that disabled children whose families lack independent financial resources do not receive the supports that would alter their developmental trajectory. The intergenerational poverty that results compounds.

### CDB Clawback
The Canada Disability Benefit, designed federally to lift disabled people above the poverty line, is functionally clawed back by Alberta through reductions in provincial supports. The net benefit to the recipient approaches zero. The federal investment is captured by the province, while the recipient remains in poverty.

### Compassionate Intervention Centres
Bill passed 2025 establishing the *Compassionate Intervention Act*. $180 million committed over three years for two purpose-built "compassionate intervention centres" in Edmonton and Calgary, each with 150 secure beds, operational target 2029. The legislation creates a legal framework for involuntary, mandatory institutional detention of individuals deemed dangerous to themselves or others as a result of addiction. Adult family members, legal guardians, healthcare professionals, and police can apply for a treatment order. A three-person commission (lawyer, physician, public/Indigenous elder member) decides. Detention authorized for 72-hour assessment, then secure care up to three months or community-based care up to six months. Minors under 18 held for the same duration as adults.

The historical residential institutions in Alberta detained disabled people on the basis of authority determinations made by people who claimed to know what was best for them. The *Act* re-establishes that framework using updated language. The mechanism is the same.

The "compassion" framing in the contemporary version mirrors the rhetorical framing of the historical eugenics movement, which presented institutionalization, sterilization, and exclusion as protective rather than coercive. Premier Smith's stated rationale — *"There is no compassion in leaving people to suffer"* — uses the same logical structure the Eugenics Board used to justify decisions made without the consent of those affected.

The population most likely to be subject to Compassionate Intervention orders intersects substantially with the AISH-to-ADAP transition population. Albertans with severe substance use disorders frequently have co-occurring mental health conditions, intellectual disabilities, and developmental disabilities. The simultaneous structural defunding of community-based income supports (ADAP) and the construction of secure institutional infrastructure (CIA centres) represents a coordinated reallocation of state resources from supporting disabled adults in community to confining them in institutions.

## Closing Section: Modern Eugenics Momentum

The piece does not end in 1972, and it does not end with the AISH-to-ADAP transition. It ends in a forward-looking momentum that the campaign expects readers to recognize.

In the two-year period from 2024 to 2026, the following has occurred in Alberta and adjacent jurisdictions:

- **Alberta** passed the *Compassionate Intervention Act*, establishing legal authority for involuntary institutional detention. $180 million committed for two new secure institutions opening in 2029. Children and adults held under the same provisions.
- **Saskatchewan** passed parallel legislation modeled on Alberta's blueprint, with involuntary inpatient detention at the Saskatchewan Hospital in North Battleford.
- **Ontario** announced study of involuntary substance use treatment within the corrections system.
- **British Columbia** reversed its drug decriminalization framework, signaling a broader retreat from harm reduction.
- **The United States**, under the Trump administration, executed an executive order facilitating involuntary hospitalization of unhoused people deemed mentally ill or struggling with addiction. The Department of Justice was directed to challenge judicial precedents protecting against indefinite institutional commitment.

The pattern is not local. The pattern is not coincidental. The pattern is the same operating principle — *those who make the decision feel they have the right to decide what is best for those not consulted first* — manifesting across multiple jurisdictions in the form of new institutional infrastructure.

Alberta is not exceptional in this. Alberta is leading.

The 1928 *Sexual Sterilization Act* was repealed in 1972 because the legal framework of explicit eugenics had become indefensible. The operating principle was never repudiated. The institutional capacity to act on the principle was reduced, not eliminated. What this piece has traced — through the structural-eugenics framework of the 1972–present period, through the AISH-to-ADAP transition, through the information asymmetry condition — is the principle continuing to operate through quieter means.

The *Compassionate Intervention Act*, and its continental siblings, mark the end of the quiet period.

The institutional infrastructure is being rebuilt. The legal framework for non-consensual confinement of people who have not committed a crime is being re-established. The same population that historical eugenics targeted — disabled people, people with mental illness, Indigenous people, racialized people, people in poverty — is the population the new framework targets, sometimes in the same words.

The compassion framing returns. The medicalized rationale returns. The institutional architecture is being commissioned now, for operation by 2029.

The principle Leilani Muir's case opened the records on did not stop operating in 1996. It went underground in 1972, operated structurally for fifty years, and is now resurfacing in legislative form across North America.

The piece does not predict what comes next. The piece records what is being built right now, in Alberta and elsewhere, while public attention is focused on the AISH-to-ADAP transition. The two are not separate stories. They are the same story.

*Consent was never the point.*

## Audience and Framing Considerations

**Audience:** This piece is for institutional readers (oversight bodies, academic disability studies, journalists, MLAs willing to engage), disability community members who already feel the dynamic but have not seen it named in this framework, and the secondary audience of allied advocates in Indigenous rights and other consultation-failure contexts.

**Tone:** Quieter and angrier than the *Compound Failure* brief or the information asymmetry report. The argument carries its own weight; the writing should step back and let the historical material do its work. The reader who knows about Leilani Muir should feel the parallel land as a chill, not as an accusation.

**Length:** Long-form. Probably 30–50 pages. This is the foundational document the campaign produces on the deepest version of its argument. It needs the room to develop the historical material, the legal framework, and the contemporary inventory in depth.

**Register:** Academic/legal, sourced rigorously, with extensive footnotes. No infographics, no callout boxes, no plain-language compression in the main document. A separate plain-language version follows.

**What it is not:**
- Not a polemic. The historical material is more powerful than any rhetorical framing the writer could add.
- Not a comparison that diminishes the historical victims of explicit eugenic violence. The argument is that the framework continues to operate, not that contemporary disabled Albertans are experiencing what Leilani Muir experienced. The continuity is structural, not equivalent in degree of harm.
- Not an argument that any individual policy designer or government employee is a eugenicist. The argument is structural and operates through inheritance of operating premises rather than personal intent.
